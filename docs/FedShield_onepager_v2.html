FedShield

Federated mule intelligence for India's banks
St Aloysius × RBIH FinTech Innovation Hackathon 2026 · Track 1
Dharani K & Thanushrie Satishkumar
Sri Sairam Engineering College

Problem

Mule money crosses banks within minutes; each bank sees only its own hop. Pooling customer data to see the chain is ruled out by bank secrecy and the DPDP Act 2023. Small co-operative banks and RRBs have too few confirmed cases to train a model at all.

Solution

MuleHunter finds mules inside a bank. FedShield lets every bank learn from the whole network, and catch cross-bank links, without moving customer data.

Each bank trains on its own data and sends only masked gradient histograms. A neutral aggregator (RBIH or the DPIP operator) decodes only their sum and returns one shared model, which each bank runs locally beside MuleHunter.AI.

Full model, held-out test: AUC 0.945 · precision 0.83 · recall 0.81 · 86% of the top 1% of alerts are mules. Leak-free features (a label-proxy KYC field was found and blocked).

Architecture: seven layers, each in the code

  1. Bank-side features and label shield. 169 MuleHunter-style features; doubtful labels down-weighted.
  2. Federated boosting with secure aggregation. Lossless vs pooled on the same bins; min-cell rule: no released cell describes < 10 accounts.
  3. Robustness gates. Peer AUC-PR probe and gradient-direction consensus exclude corrupt updates.
  4. Contribution weighting by peer AUC-PR.
  1. Hashed consortium registry. HMAC digests of rare counterparties; key held by the operator.
  2. Pattern library and versioned rounds. Emerging → confirmed → dormant → retired; full round log.
  3. Local inference and three-condition alerts. (1) high risk, (2) strong cross-bank link, (3) active I4C case; plain-language reasons in English, Hindi, Kannada; FIU-IND STR draft.

Deployment and trust

  • Operator: RBIH or the DPIP operator runs the aggregator and holds the registry key.
  • Decisions stay with banks: FedShield ranks and explains; freezes need a second approver.
  • Threat model: protected against an aggregator reading any one bank; small-cell inference mitigated by the min-cell rule; corrupt updates gated. Not covered: aggregator colluding with a bank (production: threshold secure aggregation).
  • Cost: full model ≈ 74 s for 3 banks on a laptop; upload per bank does not grow with network size; no GPU.
  • Business model: shared infrastructure; size-tiered membership with low or no cost for co-op banks and RRBs; contribution credits for banks whose data helps others.

Honest limits

  • Banks are simulated by splitting one dataset; stress tests above probe real-world differences.
  • EFRM / AML / I4C feeds are interfaces; no public data exists, so no results are claimed from them.
  • Differential privacy is costly at this scale (AUC-PR ≈ 0.2 at ε 16–64); reported, not hidden.
  • As a MuleHunter add-on, lift is clear for weaker own models and neutral where the own model is strong.
